# Visual system

## Core character

Annie's work combines four qualities:

- **Editorial clarity:** stable top bars, rules, section numbers, bilingual labels, and disciplined margins.
- **Playful authorship:** handwritten strokes, looped arrows, cut-paper objects, sticker-like highlights, and unexpected rotations.
- **Evidence-rich presentation:** finished posters, long campaign graphics, screenshots, and photographs are shown in sets rather than described abstractly.
- **Style agility:** the container stays consistent while individual projects switch among premium black, candy-bright, cyber, American-retro, and Chinese-ink moods.

The useful shorthand is **clean editorial collage with joyful motion**.

## Color hierarchy

Default portfolio frame:

- Warm ivory background: approximately `#F5F2EA` or `#F3F0E8`.
- Cobalt/royal blue structure: approximately `#3F78D8` or `#3975D3`.
- Ink black: approximately `#202020`.
- Lemon highlight: approximately `#FFF27A` or `#F4EA70`.
- Soft gray for minor copy and rules: approximately `#9A9A94`.

Use blue for navigation, section numerals, structural blobs, and handwritten gestures. Use yellow in small, high-value areas: an underline, label tab, oval shadow, or tiny tag. Keep most backgrounds neutral so varied artwork can coexist.

For project pages, let the project's native palette lead. Use the portfolio frame only as a header/footer or labeling system.

## Typography

- Pair a high-contrast display serif or condensed editorial face with a clean sans serif.
- Use very large italic or handwritten Latin initials as visual gestures, especially `P`, `C`, `T`, or section numbers.
- Set Chinese titles in a bold, compact sans serif; use smaller English translations as annotations, not equal-weight duplicates.
- Use monospaced or narrow technical labels sparingly for a catalog/editorial feel.
- Create hierarchy through scale contrast, not excessive weights: hero title, section heading, annotation, microcopy.
- Split or rotate letters only for a deliberate display word. Never compromise the legibility of essential information.

## Composition

- Work on a clear grid, then break it with one or two gestures.
- Use asymmetry: a strong object or title on one side balanced by a color blob, small label, or negative space on the other.
- Keep stable horizontal anchors near the top and/or bottom.
- Use oversized section numerals as navigation and rhythm, not merely numbering.
- Let images crop confidently. Mix one panoramic image with two or three smaller supporting frames.
- For long campaign graphics, align several narrow strips in a row and group them by mood or campaign. Vary widths slightly only when it helps hierarchy.
- Prefer crisp rectangles for project evidence and irregular cutouts for decorative or personal objects.

## Image language

- Favor clean cutouts with a faint white edge or paper-collage feel.
- Mix documentary screenshots with small tactile props such as a chair, mouse, envelope, pen, cup, toy, or analog object.
- Landscape photography favors broad horizons, vivid but believable skies, water, mountains, and travel details.
- Alternate panoramic atmosphere with closer architectural or street fragments.
- Use black-and-white or grain only when it creates a deliberate tonal pause.

## Signature devices

- Thin blue horizontal rules.
- Hand-drawn blue loops and arrows.
- Angle brackets around Chinese headings: `<个人简介>`.
- Small yellow category tabs at page corners.
- Black sparkle/star marks as punctuation.
- Organic blue corner blobs or shadows beneath cutouts.
- Yellow oval highlights and underlines.
- Oversized blue numbers with a black flourish passing through them.
- Bilingual title pairs with clearly unequal scale.

Use two to four devices per page. Using all of them at once weakens the signature.

## Avoid

- Purely centered template layouts on every page.
- Multiple saturated accent colors in the frame around already-colorful work.
- Decorative icons that have no relationship to the subject.
- Tiny screenshots presented as proof when viewers cannot inspect them.
- Random handwritten fonts, stickers, or rotations without an underlying grid.
- Making every project cute; Annie's range includes mature, dark, restrained, and culturally rooted work.
