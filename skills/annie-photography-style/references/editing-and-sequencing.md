# Editing and sequencing

## Editing principles

- Begin with exposure and white balance that respect the actual light.
- Landscape: recover sky and snow gently; use local contrast in ridges and water, not global over-sharpening.
- Golden hour: protect warm highlights and keep blue shadows clean rather than gray.
- Architecture: correct distracting perspective when it weakens geometry, but retain intentional low-angle drama.
- Monochrome: shape separation through tonal contrast; do not crush every shadow.
- Stage: control red-channel clipping and noise while retaining haze and darkness.
- Still life: emphasize texture and color relationships without turning surfaces plastic.

## Series rhythm

Build sequences with changing scale:

1. Establishing panorama or place.
2. Medium view with structure or human scale.
3. Detail, texture, or color pause.
4. Emotional light peak.
5. Quiet closing image.

For a presentation spread, mix one dominant wide image with two or three supporting crops. Align a common edge, vary scale, and leave breathing room. Do not force every image into identical frames.

## Selection test

Keep an image when it contributes at least one of: place, scale, geometry, light, color, texture, or emotional transition. Remove near-duplicates unless the change in light or framing advances the sequence.
