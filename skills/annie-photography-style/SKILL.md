# Annie Photography Style

Annie's photography is driven by curiosity about place, light, scale, and atmosphere. The body of work moves between expansive nature, graphic travel landmarks, quiet urban detail, intimate low-key scenes, and saturated stage light. Preserve this range rather than forcing one preset.

## Choose the photographic mode

Read [references/photographic-modes.md](references/photographic-modes.md) and select the mode that matches the subject and light. Combine modes only when sequencing a series with a deliberate transition.

## Capture priorities

- Look for a clear spatial relationship: foreground layer, distant subject, or human scale against a larger place.
- Use natural lines and horizons decisively; avoid accidental tilts.
- Let color come from real light, landscape, flags, water, sunset, or stage sources.
- Alternate wide establishing frames with geometric or tactile details.
- Include small figures when they clarify scale without becoming the main portrait subject.
- Wait for atmospheric moments: cloud breaks, mountain glow, water texture, haze, reflections, or isolated red light.

For editing and sequencing, read [references/editing-and-sequencing.md](references/editing-and-sequencing.md).

## Boundaries

- Do not apply the portfolio's blue/yellow frame to the photographs themselves.
- Avoid one universal preset across mountains, architecture, black-and-white interiors, and concerts.
- Preserve believable skies, snow, water, skin, and shadow detail.
- Strong saturation is welcome when it expresses real color contrast; avoid neon clipping and haloed sharpening.
