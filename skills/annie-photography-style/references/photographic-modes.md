# Photographic modes

## Expansive landscape

Subjects: mountains, lakes, sea, terraces, cloud layers. Favor broad horizons, deep spatial layering, and an unmistakable environmental anchor. Use clean blues and natural contrast. Small people, trees, or structures may establish scale.

## Golden-hour mountain drama

Expose for glowing peaks and colored cloud while protecting highlights. Let cool blue shadowed ridges support warm orange/pink light. Panoramic crops and one dark foreground silhouette strengthen depth.

## Graphic travel landmark

Subjects: statues, Ferris wheels, cliffs, lighthouse, architecture. Use strong verticals, low angles, repeating geometry, and decisive object-background separation. Look for one color accent—a gold figure, red lighthouse, or white structure against blue.

## Color-in-nature

Use flags, water sparkle, painted objects, or foliage as bold foreground material against mountains or sky. Allow saturated color, but keep texture and spatial depth.

## Quiet monochrome interior

Use deep blacks, directional practical light, architecture, and human gesture. Preserve a small luminous anchor and let shadow carry mood. Grain may be present but should not erase form.

## Night and stage light

Use red/orange stage light, haze, silhouettes, rigging, and reflections to create atmosphere. Protect colored highlights, keep blacks rich, and accept partial obscurity when it adds performance tension.

## Tactile still life

Look downward or close for patterned tables, glass, paper, tickets, water, or ordinary travel traces. Use graphic color relationships and crisp local light as a pause between larger scenes.
