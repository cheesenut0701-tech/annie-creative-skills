# Layout recipes

## Cover or closing page

- Place one oversized editorial word across the center-left.
- Overlay a handwritten blue initial or sweep.
- Add a short Chinese subtitle on a yellow underline.
- Scatter two or three personal creative objects around the edges.
- Reserve a quiet lower band for a one-line statement and essential identity.
- Echo the cover on the final page with `Thanks` or a concise closing line.

## Section divider

- Use a large blue two-digit number as the primary anchor.
- Place the Chinese section name in bold black below or beside it.
- Add a thin black rule and a small English translation.
- Balance the type with one cutout object on an irregular blue/yellow shadow.
- Keep at least half the page visually quiet.

## Personal profile

- Mix one personal photograph with a bordered speech-panel or note card.
- Divide biography and contact/basic information into clearly labeled zones.
- Use playful arrows or dotted paths to connect details, but keep personal data visually secondary.
- If publishing publicly, omit or mask phone numbers, account IDs, precise addresses, and other unnecessary identifiers.

## Campaign or operations overview

- Show full-length graphics as aligned vertical strips.
- Group strips by style or campaign and label the mood in one to four words.
- Use a neutral canvas so color differences remain legible.
- Provide a zoomed detail or one-sentence decision note when the strips become too narrow to inspect.
- Maintain a consistent page header across groups.

## Mixed design-work page

- Use one primary work at larger scale and two or three secondary works.
- Number categories with handwritten blue numerals and black flourishes.
- Put each category label inside a restrained bordered capsule or note card.
- Keep category captions short; let artifacts explain capability.

## Photography spread

- Use either one wide panorama plus two supporting images, or a three-column rhythm of differently cropped images.
- Align at least one common edge across the group.
- Alternate scale rather than applying identical frames to every photograph.
- Keep captions sparse; location, mood, or a single observation is enough.
- Use a pale yellow horizontal band only when it helps unite disparate images.

## Social-media long graphic

- Build a strong top title zone, then divide information into modular cards.
- Use a recurring navigation cue, mascot, color tab, or number system down the scroll.
- Alternate dense information blocks with visual breathing zones.
- End with a clear action, date, location, or QR area rather than decorative closure.
- Check the graphic at phone width; body text must remain readable without zooming.

## Selection heuristic

Choose the recipe closest to the content, then introduce one controlled variation. Preserve rhythm across a series by keeping headers, margins, number placement, and caption scale stable while changing image arrangements.
